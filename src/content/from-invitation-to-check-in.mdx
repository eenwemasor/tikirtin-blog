There's a moment every event organizer knows well - the one where you're juggling seventeen things at once, your phone won't stop buzzing, and you're genuinely not sure if everything is going to come together.

It doesn't have to be that way.

When your tools are set up properly, running an event feels less like barely surviving and more like directing a production. Everything flows. You know who's coming. You know how much you've made. And on the day, your team is scanning tickets in seconds while you're actually present - not buried in a spreadsheet.

This is what running an event end-to-end on Tikirtin looks like. From the moment you decide to create an event, all the way to the last person through the door.


## Stage 1: Set Up Your Organiser Profile

Before anything else, you need an organiser profile. This is your public brand on Tikirtin - the identity that every event you publish will be attached to.

Your organiser profile lives at `tikirtin.com/org/your-username` and includes your name, bio, logo, and social links. If you're an agency managing events for multiple brands, you can create separate organiser profiles for each one and switch between them anytime.

<Image width={1200} height={900} src="/content/introduction-to-getting-started/create-organiser-details.png" alt="Create Organiser form showing Name, Email, Username, Bio, and Logo fields" />

Add your social profiles too - Facebook, Instagram, X, TikTok, YouTube - so attendees who find your event can follow you directly and stay in the loop for future events.

<Image width={1200} height={700} src="/content/introduction-to-getting-started/create-organisers-socials.png" alt="Social profiles section showing fields for Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, TikTok, YouTube, and Website" />

This takes about five minutes. Do it once, and it's there for every event you ever run on the platform.


## Stage 2: Create Your Event

With your organiser profile live, hit **Create Event** from the top navigation. You'll move through a multi-step form - each section building on the last - and you can save your progress at any point and come back.

### General Information

This is where your event comes to life. Give it a title that's clear and compelling. Write a description that actually makes people *want* to be there - not just what the event is, but why it matters, who it's for, and what they'll walk away with.

<Image width={1400} height={400} src="/content/creating-event/event-title-and-description.png" alt="Title and Description fields in the General Information form" />

Set your categories and tags so your event is discoverable by the right audience on Tikirtin. Choose your timezone (critical for events with international attendees) and your currency - everything flows from there.

<Image width={1200} height={900} src="/content/creating-event/select-existing-organiser.png" alt="Select Organiser dropdown showing an existing organiser option on the event creation form" />

### Event Schedule

Next, tell the platform when and where. Tikirtin supports three schedule types:

- **One Time Event** - a single date, single location
- **Recurring Event** - the same event repeating daily, weekly, or monthly
- **Multiple Schedules** - different dates, different cities, all managed in one event

<Image width={1400} height={600} src="/content/creating-event-schedule/schedule-index.png" alt="Event Schedule step showing three schedule type options" />

For in-person events, enter your venue address manually or drop a pin directly on the map. For virtual events, paste your Zoom or Google Meet link - registered attendees will receive it automatically. Not sure about the venue yet? Set it to **To be Announced** and update it before you go live.

<Image width={1400} height={800} src="/content/creating-event-schedule/one-time-phone-manual-address-entry.png" alt="Physical Venue with Choose from Map option showing an interactive Google Map with a search bar" />


## Stage 3: Build Your Tickets

This is where your revenue strategy takes shape. Click **+ Add new ticket** and create each tier - Early Bird, General Admission, VIP, VVIP, whatever your event calls for.

<Image width={1400} height={300} src="/content/creating-event-ticket/event-ticket-index.png" alt="Event Tickets page showing an empty list and the Add new ticket button" />

For each ticket, you set the name, description, type (Free, Paid, or Donation), price, quantity, and - crucially - the exact dates and times the ticket goes on sale and closes.

<Image width={1400} height={700} src="/content/creating-event-ticket/event-ticket-type-paid.png" alt="Paid ticket type form showing price, absorb fees, absorb VAT fees, sales start and end period, and delivery options" />

Before you save, Tikirtin shows you the full cost breakdown - service fee, processing fee, VAT, what the buyer pays, and what lands in your account. No guessing, no surprises.

<Image width={1200} height={800} src="/content/creating-event-ticket/event-ticket-cost-breakdown.png" alt="Ticket Cost Breakdown showing ticket price, service fee, processing fee, VAT, cost to buyer, and payout amount of NGN 721" />

You also control where tickets can be sold - online only, at the door, or both - and whether to display the remaining ticket count (great for driving urgency on limited tiers).

<Image width={1200} height={400} src="/content/creating-event-ticket/event-ticket-config.png" alt="Sales Channel, Enable Ticket, Make Ticket Visible, and Display Remaining Tickets settings" />


## Stage 4: Add Speakers and Build Your Agenda

If your event has speakers - and most serious B2B events do - this is where you make them visible.

Create a profile for each speaker: full name, professional title, bio, headshot, and social links. These profiles appear publicly on your event page and are one of the biggest drivers of ticket conversions. People buy tickets to see people.

<Image width={1400} height={800} src="/content/creating-event-speaker/event-speaker-basic-info.png" alt="Add New Speaker form showing Name, Role/Title, Speaker bio, and Display picture upload" />

Then build your agenda - session by session, with start times, end times, and descriptions. Include every keynote, panel, workshop, networking break, and closing remark. Your attendees will thank you for it, and it signals to first-timers that this is a well-run, professional event.

<Image width={900} height={1100} src="/content/creating-event-agenda/event-agenda-create.png" alt="Add New Agenda form showing Title, Description, Start Time, End Time, and Event Dates Applicable fields" />


## Stage 5: Design Your Event Page

Your event page is your storefront. Tikirtin gives you three layout options - Full Bleed, Trio Grid, and Parallel - so you can choose the presentation that best fits your event's vibe.

<Image width={1400} height={400} src="/content/managing-media-and-theme/managing-media-and-theme-event-layout.png" alt="Select Event Layout section showing Full Bleed, Trio Grid Layout, and Parallel Layout options" />

Upload up to five featured images - venue shots, past event highlights, speaker portraits, brand visuals - whatever communicates the experience attendees are buying into.

<Image width={1400} height={300} src="/content/managing-media-and-theme/managing-media-and-theme-featured-images.png" alt="Event Featured Images section showing five Add Image slots" />

If this is a recurring annual event, the **Archives** gallery is your secret weapon. Past event photos build enormous credibility - they tell new attendees *"this has been done before, and it was great."*

<Image width={1400} height={300} src="/content/managing-media-and-theme/managing-media-and-theme-archives.png" alt="Event Archives section showing five Add Image slots for past event photos" />


## Stage 6: Configure Settings and Add Sponsors

The final step before publishing is your event settings - and there's more here than most organizers expect.

**Custom purchase email content** lets you add a personalised message to the confirmation email every attendee receives after buying a ticket. Use it to share parking instructions, dress code, what to bring, or a simple welcome message. It's a small touch that makes a big first impression.

**Custom forms** let you collect exactly the information you need at checkout - dietary requirements, company names, session preferences, T-shirt sizes. Set each form to apply to specific ticket types and choose whether buyers fill it out once per order or once per ticket.

<Image width={1400} height={700} src="/content/creating-event-settings/creating-event-general.png" alt="Settings General tab showing Event Visibility, Thank you page, and Custom Purchase Email Content fields" />

And if you have sponsors or partners backing the event, add them here. Their logos, descriptions, and website links appear on your public event page - giving them visibility and adding credibility to your event at the same time.

<Image width={1200} height={900} src="/content/creating-event-settings/creating-event-create-event-sponsors.png" alt="Add New Sponsor form showing Sponsor Name, Description, Logo upload, and Website URL fields" />


## Stage 7: Publish and Share

When everything looks good, hit **Publish Event**. Your event goes live at its own URL - `tikirtin.com/your-username/event-name` - ready to be shared anywhere.

Post it on Instagram. Send it to your email list. Add it to your WhatsApp groups. Share it with your sponsor's audience. Every click lands on a professional, complete event page - not a half-built form, not a Google Doc with a bank account number.

From this moment, Tikirtin tracks every ticket sale in real time. You can log in at any point and see exactly how many tickets have been sold, which tiers are moving fastest, and how your revenue is building.


## Stage 8: The Day of the Event

This is what all of it was building toward.

Every attendee who bought a ticket has a unique QR code in their inbox - sent automatically the moment they purchased. Your team on the ground has access to Tikirtin's check-in tools. They scan. The system validates. The gate opens in seconds.

No printed lists. No manually crossing out names. No arguing with someone who says they paid but can't find their ticket. No gate crashers getting through because your team is too overwhelmed to notice.

You can see check-ins happening in real time - who's arrived, how many are in, how many are still outstanding. By the time you take the stage or open the floor, you already know your numbers.
