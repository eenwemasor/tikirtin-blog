Most event organizers treat email like an afterthought.

They send one announcement when tickets go live, maybe one reminder a few days before the event, and that's it. Then they wonder why their ticket sales plateau after the first week and never really recover.

Here's the truth: email is still the highest-converting channel for ticket sales - higher than Instagram, higher than Twitter, higher than WhatsApp broadcasts for a list you actually own. The difference between organizers who sell out and those who don't is rarely the event itself. It's often the email sequence behind it.

The good news? You don't need to be a copywriter to do this well. You just need the right structure, the right timing, and something to say that actually resonates with your audience.

This post gives you both - the framework and the templates. Adapt them to your event, your voice, and your audience. Then send them.

Most organizers only send Phase 1 and a vague version of Phase 3. The ones who consistently sell out work all four phases - because each one is targeting a different psychological moment in the buyer's journey.

Let's go through each one with templates you can use today.

## Phase 3: The Urgency Sequence

This is your close. The goal is to convert every person who opened your previous emails but didn't buy. Be direct. Be clear about what's at stake. Don't be shy about urgency - if Early Bird is closing, say so. If tickets are running low, say so.

## A Few Things to Keep in Mind

**Personalization matters more than frequency.** One email that feels like it was written for the reader converts better than five generic blasts. Use their first name. Speak to their specific situation. Reference what you know about why they'd care about this event.

**Subject lines are everything.** Half your audience decides whether to open based on the subject line alone. Keep them short, specific, and honest. Avoid clickbait - Nigerian audiences are sharp and will unsubscribe fast if they feel manipulated.

**Only email people who opted in.** Your email list should be people who registered for your event, signed up on your website, or explicitly agreed to hear from you. Unsolicited bulk emails damage your reputation and deliverability.

**Segment where you can.** If your ticketing data tells you someone bought a VIP ticket vs a General Admission ticket, your pre-event communications can reflect that. VIP holders might get a separate email about the exclusive networking dinner. GA holders get the general programme rundown. That level of personalization makes a real difference.

**Track your open rates and clicks.** Every email sequence teaches you something. Which subject lines got the best open rates? Which calls to action got the most clicks? What time did your audience engage most? Use that data to improve the next event's sequence.
