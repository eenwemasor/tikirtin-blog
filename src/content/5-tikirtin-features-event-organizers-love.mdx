Most ticketing platforms do one thing well — they sell tickets. And that's fine, until you realize that selling tickets is actually the *easiest* part of running an event.

The hard part is everything else: managing your event page, communicating what attendees can expect, handling fees without surprises, collecting the right information at checkout, and presenting your event in a way that makes people actually excited to show up.

That's where Tikirtin goes further than most. Here are five features that event organizers genuinely love — and the real reasons why each one makes a difference.


## 1. Automated Ticket Sales Windows (Early Bird That Manages Itself)

Ask any event organizer what they dread, and "manually switching ticket tiers at midnight" is somewhere on the list. You set up an Early Bird, you announce it, and then at some point you have to go back in, close it, and open the next tier — ideally at the exact right moment, not at 2am when you remember.

Tikirtin's **Sales Start and End Period** on each ticket type solves this completely. You set a precise date and time for when each tier opens and when it closes — and the platform handles the switchover automatically.

<Image width={1400} height={700} src="/content/creating-event-ticket/event-ticket-type-paid.png" alt="Paid ticket form showing Sales Start Period and Sales End Period fields alongside price, absorb fees, and delivery options" />

Early Bird opens the day you announce. It closes at midnight two weeks before the event. General Admission kicks in automatically. VIP has always been available but in limited quantity with the count ticking down publicly. All of this happens without you touching a thing.

For organizers running multiple tiers across different channels, this isn't just convenient — it's a completely different way of operating. You build your tier strategy once, and the platform executes it for you.

**Why it matters:** Your ticket strategy should be working for you even when you're asleep. Automated sales windows mean your early bird doesn't accidentally stay open three days too long, and your General Admission doesn't go live before you're ready.


## 2. Speaker Profiles That Actually Build Hype

If you're running a conference, summit, panel, or any event with speakers, the names on your lineup are part of what sells tickets. People don't just buy access to an event — they buy access to *people*.

Tikirtin's **Speakers** feature lets you build rich, public-facing profiles for every speaker on your lineup — complete with a display photo, bio, professional title, LinkedIn, X/Twitter, and website links. These profiles live directly on your event page where potential attendees can see them before they buy.

<Image width={1400} height={800} src="/content/creating-event-speaker/event-speaker-basic-info.png" alt="Add New Speaker form showing Name, Role/Title, Speaker bio and Display picture upload" />

For multi-day or multi-session events, you can assign each speaker to specific schedule dates — so attendees know exactly which day to tune in for which speaker.

<Image width={1400} height={300} src="/content/creating-event-speaker/event-speaker-contacts.png" alt="Speaker contacts section showing LinkedIn, X/Twitter, and Website URL input fields" />

**Why it matters:** A well-presented speaker lineup is one of your biggest conversion tools. When someone lands on your event page and sees credible, detailed speaker profiles — with photos, titles, and social links — they're far more likely to buy a ticket than if they see just a list of names. Most ticketing platforms don't offer this at all.


## 3. The Agenda Builder — Your Event Programme, Publicly Displayed

This one is underrated and underused. Tikirtin's **Agenda** feature lets you build a session-by-session programme for your event, which then appears publicly on your event page.

Every agenda item has a title, description, start time, end time, and is linked to a specific event date (essential for multi-day events). Build it out fully and your attendees arrive knowing exactly what's happening and when — Keynote at 10am, Networking Lunch at 1pm, Panel Discussion at 3pm, Closing Remarks at 5pm.

<Image width={900} height={1100} src="/content/creating-event-agenda/event-agenda-create.png" alt="Add New Agenda form showing Title, Description, Start Time, End Time, and Event Dates Applicable fields" />

**Why it matters:** An agenda does three things. First, it sets expectations — attendees know what they're getting, which reduces uncertainty and increases confidence to buy. Second, it signals professionalism — a detailed programme tells people this event is organized and worth their time. Third, it dramatically reduces the "what time does it start?" DMs. Yes, really. A public agenda is also a content asset — organizers share it on social media to build anticipation in the weeks before the event.


## 4. Custom Registration Forms — Collect Exactly What You Need

Standard ticket checkout collects a name and email. That's fine for a concert. It's not enough for a corporate conference where you need dietary requirements, company names, session preferences, or T-shirt sizes.

Tikirtin's **Custom Forms** feature lets you build any form you need and attach it to specific ticket types. Forms can be configured to be filled out once per order or once per ticket — so if a corporate buyer purchases five tickets for their team, each team member fills out their own information.

The question types are flexible too: short answer, paragraph, single choice, multiple choice, or dropdown. Every question can be set as required or optional. And a live preview shows you exactly how it'll look to buyers at checkout, before you publish.

<Image width={1200} height={800} src="/content/creating-event-settings/creating-event-create-event-form-once-for-order.png" alt="Manage Event Form showing form name, activate form toggle, and questions section" />

**Why it matters:** The information you collect at registration is one of the most valuable things an event generates — and most organizers throw it away by not asking the right questions. Custom forms turn your checkout into an attendee intelligence tool. You know who's coming, what they need, and what they care about — data that shapes the event experience and fuels your marketing for the next one.


## 5. Full Fee Transparency — No Surprises, Ever

Here's one that sounds boring but is actually a huge deal: knowing exactly what you'll earn per ticket *before* you publish.

Tikirtin shows you a full **Ticket Cost Breakdown** the moment you enter a price — service fee, processing fee, VAT, cost to the buyer, and your final payout amount. All of it, laid out clearly, so you can make informed pricing decisions rather than guessing.

<Image width={1200} height={800} src="/content/creating-event-ticket/event-ticket-cost-breakdown.png" alt="Ticket Cost Breakdown showing ticket price, service fee, processing fee, VAT, cost to buyer, and payout amount of NGN 721" />

On top of that, Tikirtin gives you control over who absorbs the fees. Check **Absorb fees** and the platform charges are taken from your payout — the buyer pays exactly what you advertised. Uncheck it and fees are added on top, passed to the buyer. Same flexibility for VAT.

<Image width={1400} height={700} src="/content/creating-event-ticket/event-ticket-type-paid.png" alt="Paid ticket form showing Absorb fees and Absorb VAT fees checkboxes" />

**Why it matters:** Hidden fees are one of the biggest sources of friction and distrust in ticketing — both for buyers who get surprised at checkout and for organizers who realize post-event that they earned less than expected. Tikirtin's transparency removes that friction entirely. You know your numbers upfront. Your buyers know theirs. No one is caught off guard.
